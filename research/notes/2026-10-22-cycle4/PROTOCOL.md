# Cycle 4: observable finite-time response

Frozen before any cycle-4 trajectories, 22 October 2026. Reuses cycle-3 checkpoints without retraining or tuning. One stationary synthetic setting, not a benchmark reproduction or a claim of asymptotic instability.

## Fixed experiment

- All five saved checkpoints; seeds 0,2,3,4 are primary because they passed the previous fit gate. Seed 1 is reported as a sensitivity case. Use the exact saved model/field implementation and record its hash.
- Adapt on the first 64 saved evaluation inputs, with no labels supplied to the field. Reuse this same batch at each step: a stationary empirical-distribution stress test, not a fresh-sample online stream. Evaluate on the remaining 192 independent inputs, whose labels are evaluation-only. Normalized MSE divides by this evaluation set's total label variance (different denominator from cycle 3).
- Six configurations: input-gradient and stopped-output rules, each with fixed reference, EMA tau=1, or EMA tau=20. Unperturbed student/reference copies start equal. Student-first gradient-descent step eta=.02; EMA alpha=tau/(tau+eta); 1000 updates, cumulative time T=20. No Adam state, augmentation, source anchoring, or new supervised training. No-adaptation control is the saved checkpoint, with constant risk and unit perturbation gain.
- Primary perturbation direction: the real student component of the dominant-real-part eigenvector of the saved stopped-output/tau=1 Jacobian restricted to the 82 non-fixed coordinates. Normalize its student Euclidean norm to 1, and use it unchanged for every configuration of that seed. The direction uses no labels but intentionally targets a local growing mode; it is a stress probe, not typical random noise. If the eigenvector is complex, rotate its phase so its largest student component is real, then take the real student part. Secondary direction: one unit Gaussian vector in those coordinates, RNG seed 40000+seed, also shared across configurations. These directions are not claimed to span every direction reachable under the particular adaptation stream.
- Perturbed trajectories differ only in initial student parameters, +/- epsilon*d; their reference starts at the unchanged checkpoint. Epsilon=1e-4 and 1e-5. The base copies start matched; perturbation probes deliberately introduce a small initial student/reference mismatch. Fixed-reference comparisons keep the same teacher across both perturbed trajectories.
- Propagate each direction through the actual time-dependent discrete tangent recurrence. Use a complex-step directional derivative (1e-20) of the holomorphic NumPy field, checked against the cycle-3 full finite-difference blocks before running. This is an implementation of the ordered tangent product applied to two specified vectors, not its largest singular value. No positive starting eigenvalue is treated as a finite-time prediction.
- Record held-out NMSE, adaptation discrepancy, teacher lag, prediction changes, and parameter/tangent state every 20 primary steps. For each probe compute held-out prediction RMS sensitivity, normalized by its initial sensitivity. Compare tangent predictions to symmetric nonlinear differences (q_plus-q_minus)/(2*epsilon). Save base checkpoints and histories.
- Repeat base and tangent trajectories once with eta=.01 and 2000 updates at the same T=20 for all configurations. This is a discretization sensitivity test, not additional tuning. No extra trajectories beyond this declared budget unless a concrete implementation bug is found and documented.

## Predictions and gates

The provisional qualitative prediction, based on cycle-3 local measurements, is that tau=1 EMA produces sustained observable sensitivity amplification and that detaching the reference output amplifies more than retaining its input gradient. This prediction can fail because the underlying state moves.

1. Numerical gate: at epsilon=1e-5, relative tangent/nonlinear prediction-difference error <=1% at all recorded times, for both directions and all configurations. Check both epsilons and report errors rather than hiding failures. Validate initial complex-step field derivatives against the independently computed full central-difference blocks to 1e-7 absolute.
2. Primary amplification gate: at T=20, the primary direction has gain >=2 for at least three of four eligible seeds under stopped-output/tau=1, with fixed-reference stopped-output gain <=1.25 for the same seeds. Report every configuration and random-direction result even if this fails.
3. Rule-separation gate: primary-direction gain(stop,tau=1)/gain(input,tau=1) >=1.25 in at least three of four eligible seeds. This tests equal step/update budgets only, and cannot establish a difference beyond time rescaling on its own.
4. Discretization gate: primary final gains and NMSE differ by <=5% under the halved step, except NMSE below 1e-8 where absolute differences are reported. Failed comparisons are labeled step-sensitive; they do not support a continuous-time interpretation.
5. Separately report material risk deterioration where final NMSE >=1.25 times no-adaptation NMSE AND the absolute NMSE increase is >=.005. Do not equate perturbation sensitivity with risk deterioration or consistency decline with correct adaptation. No hypothesis of risk improvement is assumed.

No favorable direction, seed, horizon, timescale, or learning rate will be selected after results. If the main effect is absent or numerical gates fail, narrow/demote the explanation. If present, the next step is a mechanism/ablation and closest-prior-art audit, not a publication or robustness claim. This cycle has no distribution drift, retention, or recovery test.
