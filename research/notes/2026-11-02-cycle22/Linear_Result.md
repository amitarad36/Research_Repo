# Exactly when a safe linear correction exists

For a fixed source predictor w, old rows S, current rows X, and n current examples, take the same coefficient for both tasks. After imposing S beta = Sw, suppose the retained source information gives a nonempty compact convex feasible set C. Squared loss below means the empirical mean over these current examples. Predictions on the rows of S must be preserved. The true labels never enter the rule.

For a proposed h in ker(S), put p=Xw and d=Xh. Expansion gives maximum excess risk [||d||^2 + 2<p,d> - 2 min_{v in C}<Xv,d>]/n. The minimum is realized by compactness. Let Y=XC and z=proj_Y(p). The projection inequality bounds the excess at z by -||p-z||^2/n for every y in Y, with equality at y=z. Every alternate a has excess (||a-z||^2-||p-z||^2)/n against that same truth. The bound is attained, minimax-optimal, and strictly improves the baseline exactly when p lies outside Y. Any preimage of z in C preserves S. Membership of w outside C alone is insufficient if X aliases different coefficients.

With only S and its exact labels Sw, the feasible set is the whole affine fiber. For each nonzero current displacement d=Xh with Sh=0, beta=w-th is feasible for every t >= 0. Direct substitution gives excess (1+2t)||d||^2/n. Thus the supremum is infinite for each such fixed h. No finite data-derived restriction in that unobserved direction follows from these rows alone. If d=0 the excess is identically zero.

Constructed source information budget: two known feature coordinates, w=(1,0), protected S=(1,0) with label 1, plus one separate labeled source calibration reading on the known row (0,1). The recorded scalar is 2 and the measurement protocol supplies a deterministic absolute error bound of 1. These assumptions specify the fixture before current labels. Retaining the interval [1,3] requires two scalars, or the reading alone if the radius is public. The two unlabeled current rows (1,1) and (0,2) are available to every comparator. There is no real calibration instrument or claim about Boston Housing. If its error bound fails, so can coverage and the guarantee. Treating the calibration row as a second protected prediction forces h=0.

For the step to coefficient (1,k) and truth (1,u), the exact excess is (5/2)(k^2-2ku). As a function of truth this is affine, so the endpoint checks cover the interval. Projection k=1 gives worst -5/2 at u=1. Both source reset/no adaptation k=0 and center k=2 give worst 0. Projecting an arbitrary proposed direction into ker(S) only imposes old-output preservation: k=-1 already lies in that allowed update coordinate and gives worst +35/2. This comparison concerns max excess against the baseline, not max absolute risk. Independent exact rational computation from the two raw outputs checked all eight endpoint differences in less than the ten-second arithmetic budget. No current-task data, model training or adaptation runs entered the calculation.
